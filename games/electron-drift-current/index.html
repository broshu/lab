Physical constants used: electron charge e = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, electron mass m = 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ kg, copper free-electron density n = 8.46 × 10²⁸ m⁻³, resistivity ρ(293 K) = 1.68 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m. Temperature dependence is the Bloch–Grüneisen high-T limit: ρ(T) ≈ ρ_residual + (ρ₂₉₃ − ρ_residual)·T/293, with ρ_residual ≈ 1.5 × 10⁻¹¹ Ω·m for high-purity copper. Drift velocity from Drude: v_d = eEτ(T)/m. Random motion sampled at the Fermi speed v_F = √(2E_F/m) with E_F ≈ 7.00 eV — essentially T-independent because T ≪ T_F ≈ 8 × 10⁴ K. The (111) projection of fcc copper is shown with NN distance a = 0.256 nm and atoms drawn at the metallic radius a/2 = 0.128 nm, so nearest neighbours touch (2-D packing fraction ≈ 0.907). What is drawn is the Cu ion core, not the nucleus — the actual nucleus has r ≈ 1.2·A¹ᐟ³ fm ≈ 4.8 fm ≈ 5 × 10⁻⁶ nm, ~25 000 times smaller than a single pixel here and completely invisible at this scale.
